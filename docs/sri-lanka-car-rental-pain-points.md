# Pain Points of a Small-Fleet Rent-a-Car Owner in Sri Lanka

*Study date: 4 October 2026. Assumes a fleet of roughly 3 to 15 cars, a mix of self-drive and chauffeur-driven. Sources disagree on some tax figures, so verify numbers with Sri Lanka Customs or your accountant before committing capital. This is not legal or financial advice.*

---

## 1. Buying or replacing cars is the biggest problem

- **Vehicle taxes are very high and keep changing.** One guide puts the total landed cost of an imported vehicle at roughly 3x to 5x its purchase price in Japan.
- **The import surcharge is still in force.** A 50% surcharge on customs duty was introduced in May 2026 to curb foreign exchange outflows and has been extended until 31 December 2026.
- **Only newer cars can be imported.** Under the 3-year rule, only vehicles manufactured in 2023 or later are allowed for import in 2026.
- **Used-car prices are rising.** Importers warn that prices of imported used vehicles will rise after the 15% valuation reduction was removed.
- **Mid-year duty changes** hit both your purchase costs and your fleet's resale value.

## 2. Financing is much tighter

- **Loan-to-value (LTV) caps were cut on 25 May 2026.** They are now 40% for cars, SUVs, vans and three-wheelers that are unregistered or under a year old, and 60% for commercial vehicles. Cars registered for more than a year are capped at 60% of market value.
- **The caps are staying.** The Central Bank governor said the LTV limits will remain in place as macroprudential measures.
- **Growing the fleet now needs much more own cash**: 40% to 60% of each car's value.

## 3. Fuel costs and fuel-price swings

- **Pump prices were raised on 1 October 2026.** Petrol 92 is now Rs. 414 and Auto Diesel Rs. 392 per litre.
- **Diesel is subsidised.** The true diesel cost is about Rs. 750 per litre against about Rs. 407 at the pump (earlier in 2026), with the gap covered by a state subsidy. If the subsidy ends, prices could jump.
- **Chauffeur-driven packages usually include fuel**, so fuel is a direct margin risk unless your quotes carry a fuel-adjustment clause.

## 4. Demand is seasonal and exposed to shocks

- **Strong start to 2026:** 277,327 arrivals in January and 279,328 in February.
- **Then the Middle East war hit:** arrivals fell to 183,979 in March and 117,893 in April, which the tourism authority (SLTDA) blamed on the war.
- **Steady recovery since:** cumulative arrivals reached 1,642,215 by the first three weeks of September.
- **Narrow source markets:** India is the largest (418,019 arrivals January to September), followed by the UK, China, Germany and Australia.
- **Cash flow must cover the low months.** Loan or lease payments, insurance and driver salaries continue when bookings dip.

## 5. Licensing and compliance

- **Tourist transport is regulated.** SLTDA says only registered tour operators, travel agents and vehicle-rental businesses can legally run tourist vehicle fleets, with vehicles registered in the company name and insured.
- **Chauffeurs carrying tourists should be registered**, holding an SLTDA registration number and photo ID.
- **Foreign self-drive customers need a local permit:** home licence, usually an IDP, plus a Sri Lankan permit from AA Ceylon or the Department of Motor Traffic (DMT).
- **Recent permit changes:** a 24/7 DMT airport counter at BIA since 3 August 2025 issues temporary licences; AA Ceylon permits have been issued only on the basis of an IDP since 30 March 2025.
- **Renting to someone without a valid permit** is a legal and insurance exposure. Keep copies of the permit and licence in every rental file.

## 6. Insurance and damage risk

- **Rental cars carry a higher excess.** One insurer's policy book lists a first-Rs.5,000 excess for rental vehicles, against nil for private cars.
- **Hire-use cover costs more.** One estimator applies a 1.5x loading for hire and commercial passenger use (third-party calculator, not an official tariff; get real quotes).
- **Comprehensive baseline:** roughly 1.8% to 3.5% of insured value per year, so premiums rise with fleet value.
- **Damage disputes with tourists are common.** Use a clear damage waiver, a stated excess, a deposit, and photo or video checks at handover and return.

## 7. Competition and customer habits

- **Most tourists hire a driver.** Self-drive is a smaller niche, and driver hire needs no permit, IDP or conversion.
- **Ride-hailing sets price expectations for short trips.** PickMe holds about 70% market share in the Western Province; Uber is mostly a Colombo-area service.
- **Your edge** is longer, multi-day trips with a dedicated car, where apps are weak.
- **Tourist platform listings** for chauffeur tours bundle fuel, driver accommodation and meals, parking and tolls, which squeezes small operators on price.

## 8. Operations (author's judgement, not from sources)

- Maintenance and spare-part delays for imported cars
- Driver retention and conduct
- Idle cars between bookings
- Late payments from agents and travel companies
- Over-reliance on one or two booking agents

---

## Recommendations

1. **Track monthly profit per car:** rental income minus fuel (if you bear it), insurance, repairs, driver cost and loan or lease payment, divided by days available.
2. **Buy conservatively.** Favour fuel-efficient, widely serviceable models; the duty regime punishes large engines and parts availability matters.
3. **Price in fuel and tax changes.** Add a fuel-adjustment clause to chauffeur packages and review rates at each month-end fuel revision.
4. **Diversify demand:** direct online bookings, a few registered agents, corporate and local long-term rentals, and wedding or event hire to cover low months.
5. **Tighten paperwork:** standard rental agreement, handover checklist with photos, licence-and-permit check.
6. **Get a hire-and-rental insurance policy** and compare at least three quotes.

**Not covered:** income tax, VAT thresholds for rental businesses, and EV-based fleets.
